Estimated first-year cash-on-cash return

This is an estimate from assumptions, not a measured figure.

Price = multiple × revenue
NOI = margin × revenue            (default 24%: CLA 2026 survey average operating net profit)
Loan = Price × (1 − down%); annual debt service = 12 × amortized monthly payment
CoC = (NOI − debt service) ÷ (Price × down%)      All cash: CoC = NOI ÷ Price

Caveat: CLA operating profit is measured before debt service, owner pay, taxes, and an equipment reserve. Real CoC is usually lower than shown.

    Placement notes

    Advantages

    • Captive customers waiting 60–90 min a cycle
    • Many stores open long hours or 24/7
    • Card-payment and vending relationships often already in place

    Constraints

    • Minors are routinely present. A typical all-ages laundromat is not a compliant federal location.
    • A compliant setup needs a physically separate area where under-21s are never present or allowed (staffed or ID-gated entry), not just an ID-scanning machine
    • Landlord/lease use clauses and the laundromat owner's approval
    • Product liability and insurance; theft and vandalism in unattended stores
    • Cost of age-verification hardware; it does not cure the facility rule
    • Local tobacco retail licenses and flavor bans

    Laundromat + coffee hybrid

    Verify before buildout with the specific county health department (Maricopa County Environmental Services, Mohave County Environmental Health) and city planning/building. Code sections are cited for orientation. The local adopted edition controls.

    The core challenge

    A mixed occupancy has to satisfy the retail food code (AZ: 9 A.A.C. 8 Art. 1, which incorporates the FDA Food Code 2017, effective 2020; verified). It also has to meet the plumbing (IPC), mechanical (IMC), and building (IBC) codes the city has adopted. Under the IBC, self-service laundries are Group B. A food/drink assembly space with an occupant load under 50 can be classified Group B (IBC 303.1.1). At 50 or more it becomes A-2, which brings fire separation, egress, and sprinkler consequences. Verify the edition each city has adopted.

    Decision flow

    1. Prepackaged only

    Commercially prepackaged, non-TCS items (sealed pastries, bottled drinks). Maricopa: "Retail Food Establishments dispensing only commercially pre-packaged non-TCS food items are exempt from permitting requirements" (Plan Review Minimum Requirements; verified). Mohave: no equivalent exemption found. Its fee schedule lists a Retail/Packaged Food permit ($173 under 50 sq ft; $360 for 51–2,000 sq ft; 2026) (verified). So assume a permit is required in Mohave.

    2. Coffee corner

    Brewed coffee/espresso is open food handling, so a permit, plan review, hand sink, 3-compartment sink, and a Certified Food Protection Manager are needed. The cafe is an accessory use to the laundromat if it takes a small share of floor area (the threshold is set by each city's zoning code).

    3. Full cafe

    Hot food can trigger a Type I hood (grease) under IMC 507. A Type II hood covers heat/steam only. Significant floor area or seating can make the cafe a co-primary use (zoning), and 50+ occupants make it A-2.

    Separation and ventilation

    • FDA Food Code 2017 4-803.13: laundry facilities on the premises may be used only for items used in the food operation. Customer laundry must therefore stay physically outside the food establishment boundary. Ask the county to approve a demising wall/defined permit area. 4-803.11 (soiled linen storage) and 4-401.11 (washers/dryers must not contaminate food) also apply. 6-202.14 (toilet rooms enclosed) and 6-202.15 (outer openings protected) are relevant to layout. Jack's cited 6-501.110 covers dressing rooms and lockers, not laundry separation.
    • Dryer exhaust: IMC 504 (commercial dryer exhaust, independent of other systems, outdoors). The heat and humidity load matters for food areas. Keep positive pressure and a separate HVAC zone for the cafe.

    Permit checklist

    1. Zoning: confirm a coffee shop/restaurant is permitted or conditional at the address (Phoenix Zoning Ordinance; Lake Havasu City zoning code). Ask whether it counts as an accessory use.
    2. County plan review (floor plan and menu) before any construction (Maricopa, Mohave: verified requirement).
    3. City building permit (plumbing, mechanical, hood) and fire permit.
    4. Final health inspection, then the operating permit.
    5. Food handler cards for staff; a Certified Food Protection Manager if serving open TCS food (Maricopa).
    6. AZ TPT license (ADOR) for food sales.
